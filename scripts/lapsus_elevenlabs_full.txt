To understand Lapsus, you have to start near Oxford, in England. With a teenage boy named Arion Kurtaj.

Arion was autistic, and he went to a special school. In the real world, things were hard. Online, he was somebody else.

Online, he was known as White. Or Breachbase. And he was very, very good with computers.

He found his way into a corner of the internet where teenagers trade tricks for breaking into accounts. Where your reputation depends on how big your last hack was.

And in that world, White was climbing fast.

He was about fifteen years old.


The first trick he mastered doesn't need any code at all. It's called SIM swapping.

Think about how many of your accounts send a code to your phone. Your bank. Your email. Your crypto. Your phone number is the master key.

So the attacker calls your phone company and pretends to be you. Or pays someone on the inside. And asks them to move your number to a new SIM card.

If it works, every code now goes to them. They reset your passwords. And they walk straight in.

No brilliant code. No secret software. Just a convincing voice on the phone.

So how does a group of teenagers break into some of the best defended companies on the planet?
